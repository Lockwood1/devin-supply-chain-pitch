Fear one

Nothing vital gets lost.

The problem

Years of customizations, reports, and business rules that nobody wrote down. Some still do something important. Nobody is sure which, and the people who built them have often moved on.

What Devin does

Reads the legacy custom code and documents every business rule, report, and integration touchpoint before anything is decommissioned. Produces an inventory your team reviews and signs off on.

What you get

A reviewed record of what the old system actually does, so decisions about what to keep, rebuild, or retire are made on purpose.

Fear two

Prove the new system does what leadership asked.

The problem

Leadership has requirements for the new system. Every mock cutover and go-live needs the same warehouse flows proven end to end: receiving, putaway, replenishment, pick, pack, ship. Manual testing is what slips when the timeline tightens. Bad data doesn't fail in the project plan. It fails on cutover weekend.

What Devin does

Turns leadership's requirements and the documented legacy behavior into automated tests that run before every mock cutover and go-live, so the last site is tested as thoroughly as the first.

What you get

Evidence, not assurances, that the new system matches what was asked for before orders move through it.

Why it pays off twice

The ERP replacement depends on the same integrations and the same warehouse flows. What Devin builds for the WMS migration becomes the starting point for the next project.

Documentation becomes the requirements

The reviewed record of what the old system did becomes the starting point for ERP integrations.

Tests get rerun, not rebuilt

The same suites run again after the ERP switch to prove the warehouse still works.

Knowledge stays with your team

It lives in documentation and code your team owns, not in people's heads that leave when the project ends.

Straight answers

What you're probably thinking.

“We're mid-migration. We can't add anything right now.”
That's the reason to keep it small. The pilot runs alongside the program on one area, takes a few hours a week of an engineer's review time, and never touches the cutover schedule. If it doesn't help, you stop.
“My team doesn't have time to manage another tool.”
Devin doesn't need a project of its own. Your engineers assign work the way they'd assign a ticket and review what comes back as pull requests. Review takes less time than doing the work.
“Our integrator already covers testing and documentation.”
Check the statement of work. Integrators usually test their configuration, not the custom code, reports, and interfaces your team built over the years. If they do cover it, Devin can extend their coverage; if they don't, it fills the gap without a change order.
“How do I know the documentation is right? AI gets things wrong.”
You shouldn't take it on faith. Every document and test comes back as a pull request your engineers review against how the system actually behaves. In the pilot, that review is the success criterion: if your team can't sign off on it, the work doesn't count.
“We can't give an AI access to our systems.”
Every change comes back as a pull request your engineers review and approve before anything merges. Cognition is SOC 2 Type II certified. By default, Cognition does not train its models on customer data or code. It publishes a Trust Center with the details.

A bounded way to start

Not a platform decision. One area, one engineer reviewing, a few weeks, clear exit criteria.

Pilot: document one area, test one flow

Scope
One bounded area. For example, one legacy module or a single integration. Devin documents how it actually behaves and writes the tests that prove the new system handles it.
Duration
Roughly four weeks, running alongside the program. Never in its way. A few hours a week of an engineer's review time.
Success criteria
Documentation and tests your engineers sign off on, plus a test suite that runs before the next mock cutover.
Exit criteria
If your team can't sign off on the work, don't expand. If they can, the same pattern scales to the rest of the program and into the ERP replacement.
